Mughalpura sits in Lahore's north-east, its identity tied to the Mughal-era Shalimar Gardens at its edge — a UNESCO-listed heritage site that has anchored this part of the city since the seventeenth century. Around the monument, the neighbourhood runs on family life: halls and lawns fill with engagements, Milads, dinners and weddings through the week, and the old G.T. Road belt keeps relatives close.

Functions here are family occasions first. Guest lists draw from the surrounding old neighbourhoods, venues are practical — halls for the big evenings, lawns for the closer circles — and hospitality follows the generous, unhurried pattern the area has always kept.

Halls and Lawns Around Mughalpura

The area's function spaces are straightforward and family-oriented: neighbourhood halls for Milads and engagements, open lawns for the larger evenings, and banquet venues along the roads toward G.T. Road and the northern belt. The Shalimar Gardens themselves are a protected monument rather than an event venue — functions happen in the halls and lawns around them, while many families fold a visit to the Gardens into the wedding week's photo plans, arranged under the site's own rules.

Venue choice comes down to guest movement. Old-neighbourhood streets narrow toward their interiors, so approach roads are checked for caterer and crew vehicles, parking is confirmed for the guest list, and the hall or lawn is matched to the count before decor enters the conversation.

Planning Around Mughalpura

Access is the first planning note. The G.T.
