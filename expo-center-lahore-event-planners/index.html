The third crew is the one most often skipped — and its absence is what build-week improvisation actually is. What Our Planners Handle

  • Brief development. Turning "we need a presence at the show" into a dated, sized and budgeted scope, so every later decision has something to be measured against. Floor plan and schedule. Where the stall, stage, seating and registration stand, and the order they are built in, mapped against the build window the venue allows. Vendor coordination. Fabricators, AV, caterers and transport each given slots and deadlines, so they arrive as a sequence instead of a jam at the loading point. Budget tracking. The itemized estimate is the baseline; every proposed change is costed when it is raised, so the final number is never a surprise discovered at handback. Venue paperwork and hall communication. Power requests, access forms and hall rules submitted to the venue on time and tracked to confirmation — the quiet clerical layer that prevents loud problems. Run-of-show. The minute-by-minute document every crew works from on the day, from first arrival to final handback.

Planning is mostly the discipline of dates: each item above exists so that a question is answered in week two instead of hour two of the build.

Planning Timeline for a Hall Event

Every brief moves at its own pace, but a mid-sized hall event typically follows this rhythm — treat it as a working pattern, not a promise:

  • 8+ weeks out — brief and concept. The brief is written, the space is assessed and the concept takes shape while there is still room to change direction cheaply. 6 weeks — design approval. 3D renders are reviewed and approved; after this point changes cost time as well as money. 4 weeks — fabrication start. Approved designs go into off-site production, so the venue only ever receives finished parts. 2 weeks — logistics lock. Transport, crew slots and vendor confirmations are frozen, and the schedule is circulated to everyone listed on it. Show week — install and rehearsal. The install runs inside the build window, followed by a walk-through so the teams on the day know the plan rather than the idea of it.

A late brief does not stop the work; it compresses fabrication and forces scope decisions that an earlier start would have avoided. Send the dates whenever you have them and we will tell you honestly what the runway allows.

Working With Your Internal Team

Planning is a handoff sport. Marketing owns brand assets, messages and approvals; procurement owns the estimate and the payment cycle; operations often owns the guest list. We slot into that structure rather than replacing it — assets are requested early enough to be approved without a rush, and the estimate is formatted so it can circulate internally without translation.
