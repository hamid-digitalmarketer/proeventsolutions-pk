What an Expo Centre Event Company Should Deliver

Five capabilities are worth checking before any deposit changes hands:

  • Hall familiarity. The company should ask which hall and which spot your booking covers before proposing a design, because aisles, neighbours and entrance distance change what will work. A quote produced from floor area alone is a guess with a price tag. Build-window discipline. The venue allots a fixed install period before doors open. The company should show you an install plan that fits it — off-site fabrication, sequenced delivery, doors-open as the deadline — rather than promising to "manage on the day". AV capability. Screens, sound and lighting should be designed and operated by people who have run them in halls of this scale, with the equipment named in the estimate. On-site crew. A named supervisor with the authority to make calls on show days, and a crew reachable through one channel — not a different contractor for every complaint. Dismantle accountability. The build gets the attention, but the pull-out is where floors get scratched and handback deadlines missed. Ask who owns it.

None of these are technical questions; they are accountability questions, and each can be checked in writing before you sign.

Questions to Ask Before Booking

Put these five to every bidder:

Have you worked in these halls?

A useful answer names halls, spots and what the spot changed about the build. "We do lots of exhibitions" is not an answer; "the aisle side carries the traffic there, so we would open that face" is.

Who supervises on show days?

You want a named person, on site, with the authority to solve problems without phoning you for permission. If the answer is "the team", nobody is holding the clock.

How do you handle the build window?

The honest method is fabrication away from the venue and an install sequenced to the window, so hall hours go on assembly and finishing. A company planning to fabricate inside the hall is spending your window learning what it should have learned off-site.

What is included in the itemized estimate?

Line by line: materials, crew days, AV, graphics, transport, dismantle. A single lump figure hides the decisions you are paying for and makes later changes impossible to price.

Who signs off the dismantle?

The same company that built the stand should own its removal — safe order, protected floor, hall handed back as the venue expects. If dismantle is "not included", that is a cost waiting for you at the end.
