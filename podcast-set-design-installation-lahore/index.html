A podcast studio is an acoustic problem before it is a visual one. If the room echoes or traffic hum bleeds into the microphones, no camera angle and no backdrop will rescue the recording — so our podcast studio setup in Lahore starts with the room itself: its dimensions, its reverberation and the noise arriving through its walls. The look of the set is designed once the sound is under control.

Pro Event Solutions designs, fabricates and installs podcast studios as one team — site assessment, acoustic plan, custom furniture, lighting and a sound test at the end. The work sits inside our creative production services division alongside photography and TV set builds. You are not coordinating a carpenter, an electrician and an AV supplier separately; one team owns the result.

What We Build

Every build is scoped to the format — a two-host audio show and a four-guest video panel are different rooms. These are the elements that go into the work:

  • Acoustic wall treatment. Fabric-wrapped panels, corner bass traps and diffusers mounted to absorb reflected sound, so voices record dry and clear instead of hollow. In plain terms: the room stops answering back. Broadcast mic boom arms. Desk-mounted articulated arms that hold each microphone at mouth height, absorb desk knocks and keep the camera frame clear of stands and hands. Mixer chain. The audio path — microphones into a mixer, the unit that blends and balances several mics into one clean feed, then on to the recorder or computer — planned, labeled and tested so an episode never starts with troubleshooting. Multi-angle camera lighting. Key, fill and back lighting positioned for every camera angle a video podcast uses, so hosts and guests are lit correctly in wide shots and close-ups alike. Backdrop and set design. Slatted wood, fabric, acrylic logos and signage designed to read well on camera — the visual identity of the show built into the wall rather than hung on it. Turnkey creator suites. Complete rooms where furniture, treatment, lighting and cabling arrive as one installation and are handed over ready to record.

Design Choices That Matter

Room dimensions come first. The floor area decides how many seats fit and how far cameras sit from faces, and ceiling height decides what lighting the room can carry. Both are measured before anything is drawn.

Reverberation — how long sound hangs in the air after a voice stops — decides the acoustic plan. Hard, bare rooms ring; the panel layout sets how much absorption the space needs and where it goes.

Lighting angles for video podcasts are set against the actual camera positions, not a template. Lighting that flatters one camera often fails when a second or third angle is added, so the angles are agreed together.

Cable management keeps microphone, power and video cables routed through desk channels and wall routes, so nothing dangles into shot or snags a foot mid-recording. Sightlines close the loop: hosts should look at each other naturally while every seat stays framed, and the seating geometry is adjusted until cameras and conversation agree.

Build & Installation Process

  • Site assessment. We visit the room, measure it, identify noise sources — traffic, generators, air conditioning — and check power and structure. Acoustic plan. Panel placement, bass traps and door and window treatment are mapped against how the room currently sounds, and you approve 3D renders of the finished set before fabrication begins. Fabrication. Desks, backdrops and paneling are built to the approved design. Installation. Treatment is mounted, furniture positioned, lighting rigged and cabling routed — the dusty work happens before the finishing day. Sound test. Microphone levels, monitoring and a multi-camera test recording are checked with you before handover.
